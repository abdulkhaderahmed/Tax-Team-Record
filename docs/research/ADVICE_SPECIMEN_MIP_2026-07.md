# Advice specimen — management incentive plan delivery structure

**Date:** 25 July 2026
**Source:** a one-page MIP / B-share delivery-structure schematic prepared after a transaction (four delivery routes, Day 1 structure, per-participant allowance, stamp duty and IHT notes).
**Purpose:** use one real piece of paid advice to establish, concretely, what an in-house tax function has to do with it — and therefore what the product must model. This is a product-requirements document. It is not tax advice and it does not opine on the structure.

---

## 1. What the document actually is

The schematic delivers **one decision** (how management gets equity after the deal) through **four delivery routes**, sequenced against a single per-participant limit:

| Route | Instrument | Applies | Delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MIP shares under CSOP | within the £60,000 allowance | Company grants options over newly issued MIP shares; exercise price at UMV at grant; three-year holding condition |
| 2 | MIP shares, partly paid | MIP value above the allowance | subscription at UMV, small amount paid up, balance left outstanding as a debt; beneficial ownership passes on subscription |
| 3 | B shares under CSOP | residual allowance after the MIP allocation | Company grants the option; the new offshore EBT supplies the shares and retains bare legal title; exercise proceeds applied against the Founders' deferred consideration |
| 4 | B shares under JSOP | above the allowance | EBT and participant acquire jointly under one joint ownership agreement; EBT keeps initial value plus 5% p.a. carry; participant takes 0.01% of UMV at award plus proceeds above the hurdle |

Supporting Day 1 facts: a new offshore EBT created on completion holds and delivers the B shares; an existing EBT provides cash only; the Founders sell B shares to the new EBT for deferred consideration of c.£400,000 carrying interest at the official rate (3.75%); a grossed-up cash bonus funds the first £60,000 of JSOP price with an expected shortfall of c.£20,000 met by participants personally.

Indicative figures given: aggregate CSOP acquisition cost c.£670,000 (routes 1 and 3); notional loan c.£210,000 (route 2); B-share UMV £689 at award, split EBT £446 / participant £243, participant cost c.£220,000; stamp duty c.£3,300 on the Founders-to-EBT transfer.

**The document is a decision artefact.** It records a chosen structure and the conditions that make it work. It is not, and is not meant to be, an implementation plan.

## 2. Everything the delivery structure sets running

This is the point of the specimen. One page of advice generates obligations across **six tax heads**, for **four different taxpayers**, on **at least seven distinct statutory clocks**, and produces entries that land in a corporation tax computation eighteen months later.

### 2.1 By party

| Party | What they become exposed to |
|---|---|
| **The Company** | PAYE/NIC on the grossed-up bonus and secondary Class 1; employment-related securities reporting; CSOP scheme notification and annual returns; the corporation tax deduction position on shares acquired by employees, and the corresponding disallowance of the accounting charge; deferred tax on the share-based payment; interaction of EBT funding with the employee-benefit-contribution deferral rules; VAT recovery on structuring costs |
| **The new offshore EBT** | stamp duty on acquiring the B shares from the Founders; stamp duty again on transferring the beneficial interest on exercise; trust registration; whether the trust qualifies as an employee trust for IHT purposes or falls into the relevant property regime; trustee residence and UK tax on trust income and gains; classification and reporting under FATCA/CRS as a possible investment entity |
| **The Founders** | consideration and timing on the B-share sale; the deferred consideration as an estate asset; the value-for-value analysis on which the IHT conclusion depends; the effect of the reorganisation on business property relief on retained shares |
| **The participants** | employment income and notional-loan charges; elections on acquisition of restricted securities, on a fourteen-day clock; capital gains treatment and relief eligibility on exit; stamp duty on the JSOP proceeds attributable to their interest; the personal funding shortfall |

### 2.2 By tax head — and what the page does and does not reach

| Tax head | Addressed in the document | What still has to be established |
|---|---|---|
| **Stamp duty / SDRT** | yes, and well: two 0.5% charges identified on route 3, charges on route 4 proceeds, and correctly no charge on newly issued shares | which charge is stamp duty on an instrument and which would be SDRT; the notification and payment clock on each instrument; who pays; whether the register can be written up before stamping |
| **IHT** | partially: the value-for-value conclusion on the Founders' sale, and the reasoning that market value alone would not be enough | the employee-trust conditions the new EBT must satisfy to stay outside the relevant property regime; exit charges on property leaving the trust; the close-company disposition rules; the deferred consideration in the Founders' estates; the effect on business property relief |
| **Employment income, PAYE and NIC** | implicitly, through the CSOP and gross-up mechanics | the amount, timing and reporting of the bonus; secondary NIC and levy cost; whether any route creates an earnings charge on award rather than on exit; the disguised remuneration analysis where an EBT delivers value |
| **Employment-related securities** | implicitly, through the £60,000 limit and the partly paid route | scheme notification and self-certification; annual returns; restricted-securities elections and their fourteen-day deadline; the notional loan charge on the outstanding balance and its discharge |
| **Corporation tax deduction** | not addressed | whether statutory relief is available on each route, in which accounting period, at what amount, and how the accounting charge is disallowed; the position where shares are delivered by a trust rather than newly issued; the deferral rule on employee benefit contributions |
| **Deferred tax and financial reporting** | not addressed | measurement of the deferred tax asset on the share-based payment, the split between profit and loss and equity, and the resulting tax-note disclosure |
| **Corporate interest restriction** | not addressed | whether the deferred consideration interest and any funding fall into the CIR calculation and whether a return is required |
| **VAT** | not addressed | recovery on establishment, valuation and advisory costs |
| **FATCA / CRS and trust registration** | not addressed | the offshore trust's entity classification, registration and annual reporting; trust registration service |

**The gap column is the product.** Nothing in that column is a criticism of the adviser — the engagement was to design a delivery structure, and the scope statement in the letter will say so. But the Head of Tax carries every line in it, and today nothing tells them the column exists.

### 2.3 By clock

The structure creates deadlines whose lengths bear no relation to each other. This is where in-house tax functions actually lose:

| Trigger event | Consequence | Clock |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition of restricted securities (MIP shares, JSOP interest) | joint election on the securities' restricted status | **14 days** from acquisition, no extension |
| Execution of the B-share transfer from the Founders to the EBT | stamp duty notification and payment | **30 days** from execution; interest and penalties thereafter, and the register should not be written up until the instrument is stamped |
| Creation of the new offshore EBT | trust registration | **90 days** |
| Creation of the new offshore EBT | FATCA/CRS classification, and registration where the trust is a financial institution | before the first reporting period closes |
| CSOP grant | scheme notification and self-certification | **6 July** following the tax year of grant |
| Any reportable ERS event in the year | annual returns for each registered plan and for non-tax-advantaged arrangements | **6 July** |
| Payment of the grossed-up bonus | RTI reporting on or before payment; PAYE and NIC remittance | payment date; **22nd** of the following month |
| Shares acquired by employees | corporation tax deduction claimed, accounting charge disallowed | the accounting period of acquisition, filed **12 months** after period end |
| Award and exercise | valuations relied on must still hold | on each event, against the valuation's own stated shelf life |

Nine clocks, three orders of magnitude apart, from one page of advice.

### 2.4 The conditions that can go stale

The structure holds only while a set of facts holds. Each is a tripwire, not a task:

- the £60,000 allowance is a **single** limit across both share classes, applied to MIP shares first — an allocation change silently re-routes a participant from route 1 to route 2, or from route 3 to route 4, with entirely different tax consequences;
- CSOP treatment on routes 1 and 3 depends on options being held three years before exercise, subject to good-leaver and exit exceptions — a leaver event or an early exit changes the answer;
- the IHT conclusion depends on sale at market value **and** interest at the official rate; the official rate changes, so a fixed rate agreed at completion can drift below it;
- the JSOP hurdle and the £689 UMV rest on a valuation as at award — trading, contracts or a new funding round change what a later award should cost;
- the funding shortfall of c.£20,000 is assumed to be met personally; if the Company funds it instead, the analysis changes.

## 3. What this specimen proves about the product

1. **Advice is multi-taxpayer.** The current record is organisation-and-entity scoped. Half the obligations above sit with the EBT trustee, the Founders or individual participants, and the Company still carries the operational risk if they are missed.
2. **Advice is multi-head, and the value is in what it did not cover.** A structured coverage view across party × tax head, marking each cell *opined / assumed / expressly excluded / not addressed*, is the highest-signal thing that could be produced from this document, and the product cannot produce it.
3. **The unit of implementation is the event, not the document.** Completion, EBT creation, share transfer, option grant, subscription, JSOP award, bonus payment, exercise and exit each deterministically generate a known obligation set. The existing generator works from entity facts and accounting periods, which produces the recurring calendar and misses everything above.
4. **Deadlines from advice are short and non-obvious.** A fourteen-day election and a thirty-day stamping deadline are not in any annual tax calendar. They are the exposure.
5. **The record must terminate in a number.** The corporation tax deduction, the disallowed accounting charge and the deferred tax entry are the only places this structure becomes visible to the people preparing the accounts and the return, and they arrive a year later, by which time the reasoning has left the building.
6. **Value is preserved by structure, not by summary.** The £60,000 allowance ordering rule, the two stamp duty charges and the value-for-value reasoning are all things a summary flattens and a structured record keeps.

## 4. Read-across to the other service lines

The same shape holds wherever the work is paid for, which is why the product should generalise rather than special-case incentive plans.

| Service line | Unit of work | Trigger points that carry short clocks |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporate tax compliance** | a computation line item and the query raised against it | period end; return and payment dates; instalment status changes; loss and interest carryforwards; claims and elections with two-year windows |
| **Transaction tax and tax due diligence** | a finding, its quantum and the protection asked for | signing; completion; post-completion action list; the point at which warranties expire |
| **Capital allowances** | an asset or cost line and its pool | property acquisition and the fixed-asset election window; disposal values; lease events; expenditure incurred |
| **R&D** | a project and its cost categories | pre-notification for new claimants; the additional information form, which must precede the return; period end |
| **Real estate** | a property and each transaction over it | land transaction returns; option-to-tax notification; capital goods scheme intervals; annual charges on enveloped dwellings; construction scheme verification |
| **Financial services** | an entity classification and its account population | new entity or fund; new account; change of controlling persons; the annual reporting date |

Each line differs in its event taxonomy, its rule set, the questions it asks and the deliverable it produces. **None of them differs in the spine**: event → test → obligation → owner → evidence → decision → where the number lands. That is the architecture argument in section 3 of the accompanying spec.

## 5. Boundary

This analysis is used to specify software. It does not constitute advice on the structure described, does not verify the figures, and does not conclude on any treatment. Any product built from it records and routes tax work; it does not determine liabilities, and the responsibility for approving returns, meeting filing and payment deadlines and complying with PAYE, VAT and NIC obligations stays with the directors of the client company.
