# Head of Tax workflow research

Date: 25 July 2026  
Scope: UK medium-to-large enterprises and smaller large-business tax functions  
Product reviewed: Tax-Able / Quarterday advice-to-control branch

## Executive decision

The current product contains useful control infrastructure, though its entry point
is wrong for a buyer. It asks the buyer to understand entities, rules and separate
registers before showing a completed piece of tax work. A Head of Tax buys clearer
decisions, earlier warning of risk and proof that agreed treatments were
implemented. The application currently presents its database structure as its
navigation.

The recommended entry product is **tax decision operations**:

1. record a tax matter raised by the business, HMRC or an adviser;
2. collect the facts required to decide it;
3. attach research or external advice;
4. extract proposed conclusions, conditions, actions and evidence with page
   citations;
5. obtain a named human decision;
6. assign implementation work and future review events; and
7. retain the decision history and completion evidence.

Statutory obligations remain part of the record. They should appear when a
decision or compliance process needs them. They should no longer define the first
session or the sales story.

A general tax-answering assistant is a poor first product for this team. Blue J,
Thomson Reuters and Wolters Kluwer combine licensed tax content with editorial and
technical review. Tax-Able lacks those content rights and reviewer capacity. Its
more credible position is applying advice to the customer’s facts and operations,
with a human tax professional owning the conclusion.

## What the buyer is trying to achieve

A Head of Tax has several concurrent responsibilities:

| Responsibility | Operational question |
|---|---|
| Compliance delivery | Will the group file and pay accurately and on time? |
| Tax accounting and reporting | Can Finance, auditors and the Board rely on the tax numbers? |
| Business advice | Has Tax assessed a proposed transaction, contract, reward arrangement or market change soon enough to influence it? |
| Risk and governance | Can the team show how a material tax position was identified, approved, monitored and evidenced? |
| HMRC engagement | Which issues are open, what was agreed, and what information is still outstanding? |
| Team capacity | Where is specialist time being consumed and which work is waiting on another department or adviser? |
| Knowledge retention | Can another team member reconstruct why a treatment was chosen after staff or advisers change? |

The buyer’s problem crosses documents, email, spreadsheets, calls and filing
products. A deadline calendar covers only one part.

## Evidence from current research

### Administrative burden and uncertainty are increasing

HMRC’s Large Business Customer Survey 2025 covered 558 Heads of Tax or Finance
Directors and 30 follow-up interviews. Only 39% agreed that the overall tax
compliance burden was reasonable, while 37% disagreed. The level of disagreement
was the highest across the survey series. Respondents connected the burden with
cumulative reporting requirements, Pillar Two and unresolved issues. They also
wanted more operational guidance in complex areas.

Source: [HMRC Large Business Customer Survey 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-large-business-customer-survey-2025/the-large-business-customer-survey-2025-executive-summary)

### Cross-department work is slow

Tolley’s 2025 in-house technology survey reports that 32% of respondents described
communication with other departments as slow or very slow. Only 28% described it
as fast or very fast. Fifty-seven percent identified embedding technology as the
slowest-moving area for in-house tax. The report describes tax teams working more
closely with Finance, IT and operations while their tooling remains difficult to
embed.

Source: [Tolley In-house Tax Technology Report 2025](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/tolley/tax/insights/in-house-tax-technology-report-2025/index.html)

### Most departments remain reactive

The Thomson Reuters and Tax Executives Institute 2026 technology survey covered
170 US corporate tax technology decision-makers. Sixty-four percent placed their
department in the chaotic or reactive stages of the report’s maturity scale.
Thirty-four percent reported constraints in both budget and implementation
resources. Thirty-two percent addressed capacity through process improvement
without additional hiring or technology investment.

The sample is US-based, so the percentages should not be presented as UK market
estimates. The operating pattern is still useful: buyers need a narrow
implementation with visible savings rather than a broad transformation programme.

Source: [2026 Corporate Tax Department Technology Report](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/03/2026-Corporate-Tax-Technology-Report.pdf)

### Data collection consumes specialist time

Thomson Reuters states that corporate tax professionals may spend up to 80% of
their time collecting, entering, checking and consolidating information during
filing work. Vendor figures require caution, though the described workflow matches
the recurring market pattern: tax asks Finance, Payroll, HR and local teams for
data; checks it; reformats it; and follows up.

Source: [ONESOURCE DataFlow](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/en/onesource/workflow-manager/dataflow)

### HMRC assesses systems, processes and governance

HMRC’s Business Risk Review material assesses whether a large business has the
systems, processes and resources required to deliver the right tax at the right
time. HMRC’s published tax-strategy guidance asks qualifying groups to describe
how they identify tax risk, their governance arrangements, Board oversight and
the systems and controls used to manage risk.

Sources:

- [HMRC BRR+ systems and delivery guidance](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/tax-compliance-risk-management/tcrm3342)
- [HMRC large-business tax strategy guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/large-businesses-publish-your-tax-strategy)
- [HMRC Senior Accounting Officer certification guidance](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/senior-accounting-officers-guidance/saog15200)

These requirements support a decision and control record. They do not prove demand
for a particular software product.

## Pain-point ranking

The following ranking combines buyer frequency, economic impact, current product
fit and the ability to validate with accessible documents.

| Rank | Pain | Why it is expensive | Product opportunity |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tax learns about business changes too late | Remediation costs rise after contracts, payroll, transactions or systems are committed | Structured tax-matter intake with fact requests and decision dates |
| 2 | Agreed advice is difficult to implement and monitor | Conditions stay in PDFs; actions move to email or personal lists; future events are forgotten | Advice review that creates owned actions, evidence and tripwires |
| 3 | Information requests require repeated chasing | Tax specialists spend time administering requests; incomplete data compresses review time | Department-facing requests, reminders, escalation and readiness |
| 4 | Decisions are hard to reconstruct | Staff turnover and adviser changes remove context; audits require manual archaeology | A decision record linking facts, sources, reviewer, approval and evidence |
| 5 | The Head of Tax lacks one view of material work | Calendars show dates; task tools miss tax conditions and source lineage | Attention queue organised by exposure, decision and blocked work |
| 6 | Regulatory change must be converted into company action | Research identifies a change; teams still need to assess facts and implementation | Change-impact matters with entity scoping and recorded dispositions |
| 7 | Compliance calendars require maintenance | Entity changes and regime rules create spreadsheet upkeep | Controlled rules and imports, retained as a supporting capability |
| 8 | Technical research is slow | Multiple sources and repeated source checking consume time | Integrate or link trusted research; avoid owning general tax content initially |

## Market categories and the remaining opening

### Filing and calculation

Alphatax, ONESOURCE Corporate Tax and CCH Integrator calculate, validate and file.
They hold tax logic and often integrate with ERP or reporting data. Replacing them
would require wide tax-content coverage, calculation accuracy, filing support and
customer migration.

Sources:

- [Tax Systems Alphatax](https://www.taxsystems.com/what/corporate-tax-software/)
- [Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/en/onesource/)
- [Wolters Kluwer CCH Integrator](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en-gb/solutions/cch-integrator)

### Compliance operations

Loctax, ONESOURCE Workflow and similar products centralise obligations, documents,
responsibility and status. Loctax gives Heads of Tax global compliance visibility.
ONESOURCE Workflow combines checklists, information requests, documents, entity
data and audit history.

Sources:

- [Loctax compliance software](https://www.loctax.com/tax-compliance-software)
- [ONESOURCE Workflow Manager](https://mena.thomsonreuters.com/en/products-services/tax-accounting/workflow-manager.html)

Tax-Able will struggle if its principal claim remains “all obligations in one
place.” That claim already has established suppliers.

### Tax research and answer generation

Blue J, CoCounsel Tax, Tolley and CCH answer technical questions using owned or
licensed content. Their content supply, citation verification and editorial
maintenance create a high entry cost.

Sources:

- [Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/how-it-works-brand)
- [CoCounsel Tax](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/en/products/cocounsel-tax)

Tax-Able should allow a user to attach research from these products to a matter.
It should record the resulting company decision and implementation.

### Managed compliance for simple companies

Inkle offers US startups bookkeeping, filings, deadlines and human support in one
service. Its clarity comes from a narrow customer, a known filing set and an
included service team. It is a useful lesson in product packaging. Its customer
and delivery model differ from a UK enterprise tax function, so it should not
define the Tax-Able roadmap.

Source: [Inkle](https://www.inkle.ai/)

### General controls software

Products such as Impero manage finance and tax controls. They can model risks,
controls and testing without understanding tax advice or retaining page-level
adviser provenance.

Source: [Impero](https://www.impero.com/)

### Product opening

The proposed opening is a **tax matter and decision record with advice
implementation**. It connects areas that are otherwise split:

`business event → facts → technical source/advice → decision → actions → evidence → later review`

Each adjacent supplier covers parts of this chain. Tax-Able can specialise in the
chain itself, use existing filing and research products as sources, and retain the
company-specific decision history.

## Why general “real tax advice” should wait

An answer product needs:

- current legislation, case law, HMRC material and interpretation;
- content rights that permit retrieval and answer generation;
- tax specialists who review topic coverage and updates;
- jurisdiction and tax-type boundaries;
- answer evaluation against difficult factual scenarios;
- professional-liability controls; and
- a distribution route that can displace existing research subscriptions.

The user’s access to Tolley and Practical Law can support private review at work
within employer and licence terms. It cannot supply a commercial product corpus
unless the relevant rights holder grants that use.

The advice-implementation product has a narrower claim:

> The system identifies statements explicitly present in a supplied document,
> keeps their conditions and citations attached, and helps the customer review
> and implement them.

The customer or adviser retains responsibility for the tax conclusion.

## Defensibility

The model provider supplies a replaceable component. Installing an open-source
model does not create durable advantage by itself.

The defensible assets are:

1. **A company-specific tax decision graph.** Each decision connects business
   facts, advice versions, conditions, owners, actions and evidence.
2. **Reviewed extraction data.** Human dispositions show which passages produced
   material controls, which qualifiers mattered and which candidates were noise.
3. **Matter templates.** Employment tax, valuations, transactions and HMRC
   correspondence develop repeatable fact requests, approvals and evidence
   expectations.
4. **Operational history.** The product learns where work stalls, which inputs
   recur and which business events create tax work.
5. **Embedded handoffs.** Finance, Payroll, HR, Legal, advisers and approvers use
   the same matter record. Switching removes accumulated context and completion
   evidence.
6. **Trust measurements.** Citation accuracy, condition preservation, reviewer
   acceptance and time saved are measured by document type.

Tax rules can add value where deterministic dates or scoping questions are safe.
They are one asset among several.

## Initial customer definition

Entity count is a weak primary filter. A 60-entity dormant property group may be
easier than a 5-entity business with mobile employees, share plans, acquisitions,
outsourcing and frequent adviser input.

Use these buying signals:

- one to fifteen people performing in-house tax work;
- regular use of external tax advisers;
- five or more material tax matters a month;
- several tax-sensitive stakeholder handoffs;
- reliance on email, SharePoint and spreadsheets to track advice;
- recurring employment tax, transaction, transfer pricing or HMRC matters; and
- an existing filing engine that the product can complement.

A sensible initial UK customer may have £100 million to £2 billion of revenue and
five to fifty active entities. These are discovery bounds rather than product
limits. Complexity and advice volume should determine qualification.

## Product operating model

### Core object: Tax Matter

Every significant piece of work should begin with a matter containing:

- question or business event;
- tax types and entities potentially affected;
- materiality and decision date;
- business sponsor and tax owner;
- known facts, requested facts and unresolved conflicts;
- source documents and research;
- proposed treatment and alternatives;
- named reviewer and approver;
- final decision with rationale;
- implementation actions;
- evidence and approval gates;
- future review events; and
- links to any resulting filing obligation.

The current schema has actions, assumptions, caveats, tripwires, exceptions,
evidence, requests, approvals, documents and obligations. It lacks the parent
matter and the explicit decision. Adding more standalone registers before those
two objects would increase user confusion.

### First-session path

A useful first session should require only:

1. a matter title;
2. a source document or a plain-language question;
3. an optional entity; and
4. a decision date.

The application can request more entity or registration data if a rule or filing
workflow needs it. Users should receive a reviewable output before completing a
group profile.

### Head of Tax home screen

The home screen should answer:

- What could cause loss, penalty or delay?
- Which decisions need me?
- Which matters are waiting on Finance, Payroll, Legal or an adviser?
- Which accepted treatments have not been implemented?
- Which deadlines are approaching with missing evidence?
- Which material positions changed since the last review?

Counts of entities and stored records are administration measures. They should
sit in settings or reports.

## Current repository assessment

### Already implemented

- OpenAI API usage through structured chat-completion output;
- page-aware PDF text blocks and server-side quote verification;
- OCR and durable extraction jobs;
- a global human review queue;
- actions, assumptions, caveats, tripwires, exceptions and evidence;
- requests, reminders and independent approvals;
- document versions, access restrictions and audit events;
- controlled UK rule versions and impact review; and
- a benchmark harness with accuracy claims disabled until review is complete.

### Why it appeared absent

- the unavailable hosted database prevented the demo from opening;
- the home screen centred entities and obligations;
- twenty-plus navigation entries exposed internal record types;
- the AI process was several screens away from the first page;
- the seed supplied an entity and source systems without a completed matter;
- no parent matter connected extracted advice to the implementation record; and
- the interface offered no guided sample that worked without infrastructure.

### Changes made in this research sprint

- replaced the landing screen with a Head of Tax workbench;
- ordered work around advice review, blockers, overdue requests and approvals;
- presented recent source documents as advice-implementation matters;
- retained deadlines as a supporting panel;
- reduced the primary navigation to work and buyer-facing records;
- moved specialist registers and settings into a secondary disclosure;
- added a database-independent guided EOT valuation review; and
- added a graceful sample workspace when live data cannot load.

## Build sequence

### Proof stage

1. Test the guided workflow with five in-house tax professionals.
2. Record whether they can explain the product after three minutes.
3. Ask each participant to map a recent tax matter into the proposed fields.
4. Annotate five employment-tax or valuation documents with two independent
   reviewers where available.
5. Measure material-control recall, qualifier preservation, citation accuracy,
   false positives and review time.

### Product stage

1. Add `TaxMatter` and `TaxDecision` to the domain model.
2. Make “new matter” the principal creation path.
3. Attach current control records to a matter rather than presenting them as
   separate destinations.
4. Add a fact-request portal or low-friction response link for business users.
5. Add email and Microsoft 365 intake after the manual matter flow works.
6. Add matter templates for valuation and employment-tax advice.
7. Add management reporting for ageing, waiting time, adviser spend, exposure
   and implementation completion.

### Later stage

- regulatory-change feeds that create impact matters;
- controlled research integrations;
- filing-engine and ERP links;
- model routing by document type;
- private model deployment where customer security requires it; and
- further jurisdictions only after customer demand funds content review.

## Validation questions

The next interviews should test observable past behaviour:

1. Tell me about the last tax question that arrived from outside the tax team.
2. When did Tax learn about it, and which decision had already been made?
3. Where were the facts collected?
4. Where is the final tax conclusion stored?
5. How do you know every condition in the advice was implemented?
6. Which person follows up with Finance, Payroll, HR or Legal?
7. What happens when the adviser issues a revised report?
8. How would another team member reconstruct the decision in two years?
9. Which open tax matters does the CFO ask about?
10. What evidence would HMRC or the auditor request?
11. Which part consumes the most senior tax time?
12. What failed or nearly failed in the past year?

Positive feedback is weak evidence. Ask for redacted examples, spreadsheets,
emails, trackers and elapsed time. A paid pilot should replace one active matter
log or advice tracker, while existing filing tools stay in place.

## Stop conditions

Reconsider the product if:

- users have fewer than three material advice matters per month;
- they can reconstruct advice implementation easily from existing tools;
- they refuse to store source advice or decision rationale in a shared system;
- reviewer time stays equal to manual document reading;
- source citations fail the agreed benchmark;
- the buyer wants calculation and filing more than decision operations; or
- workflow vendors already meet the need at an acceptable cost and setup effort.

## Working product statement

**Tax-Able gives an in-house tax team one record of each material tax matter:
the facts requested, advice received, decision approved, work assigned and
evidence retained.**

The product complements research subscriptions and filing engines. Its value
comes from getting tax advice implemented and keeping the reasoning available
when the team, adviser or business changes.
